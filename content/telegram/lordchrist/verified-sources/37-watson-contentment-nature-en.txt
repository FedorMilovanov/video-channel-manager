Verbatim (contiguous excerpt):

Holy contentment is a divine thing, a fruit of the Spirit (Gal. 5:22). Nature may produce an outward calm, a stoic apathy; but a meek, quiet, submissive frame of heart under every condition, every loss, every trial, is a fruit from above. It is no dead insensibility, no dull uncomplaining patience; it is a lively, sweet submission to the will of God.

Contentment is an intrinsical thing; it is not without, but within the heart. The Christian carries a heaven in his own bosom. Outwardly he may be in straits, but within he has that melody which the world hears not; he may be stripped of comforts, but not of consolation. Contentment is no gift of fortune, but of faith. And so the apostles saying, I have learned in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content, is a speech worthy to be engraven upon our hearts.
