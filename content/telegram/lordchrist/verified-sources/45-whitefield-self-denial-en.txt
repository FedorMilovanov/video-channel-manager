Verbatim (contiguous excerpt):

If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me (Luke 9:23). These words of our Lord are not optional counsel for those who want to be extra holy; they are the universal law of his kingdom, binding upon every soul that names the name of Christ. There is no discipleship without self-denial, for such was the Master himself: he denied himself, took upon him the form of a servant, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.

Self-denial does not mean bodily mortification for its own sake, nor sourness, nor quitting all lawful comforts, nor flight from the world into a wilderness. It means the dethronement of self as our rule and end: putting away our own righteousness, our own will, our own glory, our own lusts, and the lusts of the world, that we might live unto him who died for us and rose again. It is ceasing to be lord over ourselves and owning Christ as sole Lord.

Take up thy cross daily. Not a heroic feat once done, but constancy in the ordinary round -- in small temptations, in household vexations, in being overlooked, in labour, in prayer, in forgiving injuries, in faithfulness to duty. He that does not carry his cross in the common days will not stand in the day of great trial. And while you bear it, remember: you do not bear it alone; Christ bears you with your cross, and he that called you is able to keep that which you have committed unto him against that day.
