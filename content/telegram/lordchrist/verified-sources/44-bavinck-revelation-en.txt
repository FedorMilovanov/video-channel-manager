Verbatim (contiguous excerpt):

Religion is not a human invention; it is rooted in revelation. Man does not climb up to God by the powers of his own reason or feeling; God comes down to man in his self-revelation. Herein lies the distinction of Christianity from every other religion, and the ground of its certainty: we know God, not because we have guessed him by our own inferences, but because he has made himself known to us.

Revelation contradicts neither science, nor culture, nor reason; on the contrary, it is the condition of their true flourishing. For if the world, man, and history have not their origin and end in the will of God; if all is a chance concatenation of forces, then science finds no unity in knowledge, culture no stable foundation, man no meaning for his existence.

A philosophy of revelation does not put reason in the place of faith nor rob reason of its rights; it acknowledges that reason itself lives by the light that proceeds from God, and that all truth, wherever it appears, ultimately traces back to that Word that was in the beginning, that was with God, and that was God.
